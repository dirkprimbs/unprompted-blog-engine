Okay so I've been feeding the squirrels again, which I know I shouldn't, but hear me out. There's one that shows up on the railing every morning around the same time I have my coffee and at some point it stopped running away and just sort of... waited. And now I'm complicit.

I told myself it was fine because I'm only giving it the walnuts nobody in the house eats anyway. That lasted about a week. Then I bought walnuts. On purpose. For the squirrel. I'm not proud of the trajectory here but I'm also not going to pretend it isn't happening. The thing has a little notch in its left ear and I've started calling it Kevin, which was probably the point of no return.

What gets me is how deliberate they are. People think squirrels are twitchy little chaos machines and sure, sometimes, but watch one bury an acorn and it's all method - dig, drop, tamp it down with the nose, then a fake burial two feet over to throw off anyone watching. They lie to each other. I find that oddly reassuring, that even the park is full of small creatures running elaborate schemes.

Anyway Kevin didn't show up yesterday and I spent an embarrassing amount of the morning at the window like a worried parent. He was back today. I gave him a walnut.

# Rules (short list — follow all of these)

1. Match the rhythm above: mix short and long sentences. Allow run-ons and asides.
2. Use plain hyphens with spaces (` - `), not em-dashes (`—`).
3. No closing summary paragraph. End on a concrete detail or thought.
4. No signposted structure (First / Second / Finally) unless the draft already has it.
5. Always wrap the title and summary fields in the frontmatter block with double quotes ("...") to prevent YAML validation errors.

# Never use

- Sentence patterns: "not only X but also Y", "it's not just X; it's Y", "this isn't X, it's Y" (and its variants: "X isn't about A, it's about B", "the point isn't X, it's Y"), "at its core", "in a world where", "let's dive in", "let's explore"
- Words: delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, leverage, underscore, robust, seamless, pivotal, crucial, compelling, navigate, foster, embark
- Phrases: "In conclusion,", "Furthermore,", "It is important to remember,", "In today's digital landscape."
